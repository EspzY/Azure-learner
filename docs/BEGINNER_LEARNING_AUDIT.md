# Beginner learning audit — 3.1A

Candidate design review, 2026-10-25. Baseline `b4f8152b0a901cc8c6c8b99eb588c3f49cd9f064`. This is an implementing-author audit of code, original manuscripts and rendered `/learn/computing?topic=capacity`, not a novice study or SME approval. Production content/evidence is preserved. [Blueprint](FOUNDATIONS_PEDAGOGY_BLUEPRINT.md) owns the proposed correction; [storyboard](FOUNDATIONS_31A_STORYBOARD.md) owns exact teaching.

## What actually exists

The course correctly orders the three official modules, exposes six substantial original computing investigations, retains a changed-context assessment and shares the real Portal inventory. This is more than a glossary. The problem is instructional prerequisites and composition, not absence of every explanation. Source review, structural completeness, implemented activities, learner evidence and comprehensibility are separate axes. Earlier claims of a complete local implementation candidate did not establish beginner suitability.

The official module explicitly assumes basic IT familiarity. Our target adult only knows everyday computer/browser use. Bridging that difference is original AzureLab prerequisite teaching, not an additional official objective or exam-readiness claim. L01/M02/CR01 were re-read 2026-10-25; six module objectives and three-module order remain. The exam overlay additionally names serverless, still outside this module.

## Rendered evidence and method

Additional evidence limit: `correctPractice()` in `foundations.ts` and `UnitInvestigation` preserve a prediction even when wrong, but the capacity guided predicate primarily checks required probe witnesses and the service-provisioning response. This is legitimate guided participation, **not** a test that the numeric mental model improved. Do not “fix” historical records by discarding wrong guesses; the candidate instead requires a causal explanation plus separately versioned changed-task criteria.

Baseline captures: [reading](screenshots/31a-before-reading.png), [investigation](screenshots/31a-before-investigation.png). A clean Chromium context at 1440×900 opened the real production route, waited for its heading and scrolled to the actual Run control. No learner save was imported. The first frame shows the correct official module twice, four glossary definitions and substantial text before a model. The investigation frame shows stacked selects/number field and the already-explained service-provisioning response. These support A01–A06/A09; they do not establish that every learner fails.

Existing tests correctly verify arithmetic, required probes, preservation, first/help/retry and the operational workflow (`foundations.test.ts`, `integrity.test.ts`, `foundations.spec.ts`, `course.spec.ts`). They do not test whether “plass” is understood, whether the picture supports explanation, or whether the guided answer was inferred rather than copied. Keep their safety assertions; add new design-proof tests without weakening them.

## Priority and explicit non-fixes

First establish physical computing and one request; next justify the unit and one-period model; then separate worked/predict/observe/explain. Naming is a just-in-time Portal bridge, not an opening glossary. Benefits remains unavailable; specialist content stays on the roadmap. No extra service, mascot animation, general-purpose rendering engine or expanded quiz catalogue resolves these findings.

Human review must ask the learner to explain what each object and number means before they run anything. Stop and record the exact misconception if they cannot; do not coach the target answer and record that as success. [Storyboard review gates](FOUNDATIONS_31A_STORYBOARD.md#review-and-phase-31b-handoff) define the novice/IT/SME exercises.
