# Roadmap

Version: Phase 1A proposal, 2026-10-22. This supersedes the earlier feature-oriented Phase 1 roadmap. No schedules or whole-curriculum delivery promise. Completion means meeting the stated learning/engineering/human gates; status and actual checks live in [PROJECT_STATUS.md](PROJECT_STATUS.md). Phase 0/0.5 and the bounded Phase 1A.5 expedition are implemented; none is evidence of a complete educational product.

## Phase 1A — product and learning architecture

- **Learning outcomes:** specify a connected foundation-to-professional graph and independent evidence for applying each area; do not claim learner outcomes have been observed.
- **User experience:** reviewable journey, representation patterns and three detailed examples, including persistent-world continuity and beginner/experienced entry.
- **Functional scope:** product alternatives, selected model, competencies, sources/credential overlay, six system boundaries, first chapter inputs/flow/criteria and professional review rubric.
- **Technical deliverables:** the five mandatory blueprint documents plus focused example/source registers; update engineering continuity and require them in docs:check. Runtime remains Phase 0.5.
- **Automated verification:** required-file/link/heading/image checks, negative documentation fixtures, formatting, repository diff/secret integrity and required toolchain checks for the small docs-gate change. No new interaction tests can establish an unimplemented chapter.
- **Human review:** owner reviews product choice, Chapter 01 scope and unresolved risks; planned Azure SME, learner and accessibility exercises are explicitly unperformed.
- **Acceptance:** every major capability identifies learning/action/state/evidence; authoritative dated sources and exact example rules exist; no proposed feature is described as shipped; Phase 1B target is bounded enough to implement without reinventing it.
- **Exclusions:** full Phase 1B implementation, frontend rewrite, new services, accounts, Azure calls, production deployment. Owner review is the gate to the next phase.

## Phase 1A.5 — playable expedition foundation

- **Learning outcomes:** compare service responsibilities, make a requirement-based service choice, retain SaaS data/access duties and apply organization concepts in the existing sandbox.
- **Experience/scope:** one continuous route, four explanation depths, two distinct side-route checks, one actual portal/world and exact return/resume; future regions unavailable.
- **Technical deliverables:** typed route/knowledge definitions, interruptible perspective camera, shared validated snapshot/command replay, unchanged legacy rules/awards, documentation and traceable browser evidence.
- **Automated verification:** 48 unit tests plus three documentation fixtures, production desktop/mobile journey and legacy E2E/axe, type/lint/format/docs/build, dependency/secret/request checks; actual results in PROJECT_STATUS.md.
- **Human gates:** screenshot inspection performed by Codex; learner, Azure SME, physical device and human assistive-technology reviews pending. Engineering passes do not establish learning effectiveness.
- **Acceptance:** actual teaching state change, wrong/correct detours and exact return, real resource creation and persistent resume, no duplicate awards/false completion, usable keyboard/reduced-motion/mobile alternatives.
- **Exclusions:** full Chapter 01, new service engines, independent assessment, backend, production deployment and claimed mastery. This explicitly authorized phase is not permission for uncontrolled Phase 1B expansion.

## Phase 1B — one complete playable chapter

- **Learning outcomes:** apply service responsibility, identity/scope, independent metadata/resource location, hosting dependency and a filtered policy constraint on an unfamiliar variant (F01/F02/G01/G02/G03/C01 introductory outcomes).
- **User experience:** [Chapter 01](CHAPTER_01_BLUEPRINT.md): responsibility matrix → scope/world construction → prediction → meaningful denial/correction → plan/app → independent transfer → explicit checkpoint promotion. Experienced entry avoids a forced tutorial.
- **Functional scope:** one chapter, guided fixture and two changed transfer fixtures, four domain command types, narrow plan/app models, one custom deny subset, criterion findings and local world/evidence envelopes. Keep legacy activity behavior available through an adapter.
- **Technical deliverables:** typed/versioned scenario data, pure transitions/receipts, small stage runner, equivalent native controls, supported model boundaries, local migration/reset/resume/fork/promotion and idempotent participation. Avoid premature generic registries.
- **Automated verification:** V01–V16 plus valid/invalid schemas, deterministic replay/duplicate commands, policy applicability, dependencies, no unsupported success, hints/variant exposure, migration/XP/storage conflicts. Required type/lint/format/docs/unit/build gates; production desktop/mobile/keyboard/reduced-motion E2E and axe; zero unintended external requests; inspect scene/matrix/denial/result screenshots and benchmark proposed budgets.
- **Human review:** beginner and experienced-engineer transfer tasks, Azure administration review of rules/omissions, physical mobile and human screen-reader/keyboard review. Record task observations, assistance and unresolved misconceptions with consent; no analytics assumed.
- **Acceptance:** all five chapter criteria are observable; a correct unfamiliar response can be classified independently of guided completion/XP; rejected operation cannot create resources; a learner can explain the changed location outcome; checkpoint survives supported resume without corrupting replay. Professional rubric categories at least 2, no unresolved blocker. If human reviews cannot be performed, report the chapter as an engineering candidate with learning/accessibility gates pending, not a validated educational release.
- **Exclusions:** B/C full experiences, networking/identity engines, full Bicep/ARM runtime, mastery/readiness percentages, accounts/backend, curriculum expansion or live Learn/Azure.

## Phase 1C — reusable platform validated by authoring

- **Learning outcomes:** demonstrate the same concepts in a second bounded problem and establish that reasoning transfers across representation/fixture changes; preserve evidence quality.
- **User experience:** a second authored scenario can consume a checkpoint or reference fixture without new mission-specific JSX. Choose the case from Phase 1B friction; a narrow new-connection NSG trace with fixed DNS/routes is a candidate, not the full B incident.
- **Functional scope:** extract only demonstrated common domain/scenario/content/rendering/assessment/progression boundaries; validate one additional bounded model if needed. No universal Azure simulator.
- **Technical deliverables:** scenario/model registries, reference/authoring validation, source/version compatibility policies, typed view adapters and consistent evidence/progression interface as specified in [SIMULATION_PLATFORM.md](SIMULATION_PLATFORM.md).
- **Automated verification:** unknown capability rejection, reference/cycle/reachability checks, feasible solutions, cross-scenario isolation, replay/old-version compatibility and same commands/evidence across views. Preserve all Chapter 01 regressions and CI gates.
- **Human review:** an engineer authors the second package from the contract; Azure reviewer challenges counterexamples; learners explain causality in the changed problem. Record what required code versus data changes.
- **Acceptance:** a reviewed second scenario reuses orchestration/views/evidence without duplicated mission branches; new model has source-backed positive/negative fixtures; old attempts remain intelligible; no claim that schema validation proves pedagogy.
- **Exclusions:** full catalog/schema migration platform, arbitrary plugins/interpreters, real cloud execution, full B/C, shared multiplayer state or speculative microservices.

## Phase 2 — expand the engineering universe one experience at a time

- **Learning outcomes:** configure and independently diagnose networking, compute, data, identity and telemetry; recover without overbroad permissions or isolation bypasses.
- **User experience:** progressively extend the persistent service environment, fork incidents and compare evidence. B in [EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINTS.md](EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINTS.md) is a candidate after its prerequisites/models exist. Offer branches and reference checkpoints instead of one rigid course.
- **Functional scope:** prioritize one validated scenario/model set per increment across N/C/D/I/O/A; add delayed unfamiliar retrieval and transparent practice recommendations. Select breadth from learner misconceptions, not service-count targets.
- **Technical deliverables:** reviewed bounded service models and synthetic observation producers, source/version updates, retention metadata and authoring fixtures. Evaluate storage needs from actual usage; backend/auth requires separate approval.
- **Automated verification:** rule-level valid/invalid/counterexample fixtures, new-connection/access/recovery traces, replay, negative controls, evidence/assistance and all required browser/accessibility/performance gates. Verify telemetry originates from modeled causes.
- **Human review:** domain SME plus independently solving learners, including relevant accessible/mobile paths. Test delayed transfer rather than declaring retention from an immediate retry.
- **Acceptance:** each release shows what was learned, changed and independently applied; a common false mental model is tested; checkpoint compatibility and explicit omissions are respected. Do not average away a failed technical/evidence category.
- **Exclusions:** implementing every service, automatic real resources, broad enterprise rollout, paid/external services without authorization, unsupported credential coverage claims.

## Phase 3 — advanced design, specializations and optional real practice

- **Learning outcomes:** defend architecture/migration/operations/security/data/AI/platform tradeoffs using constraints, failure evidence and uncertainty (R/X tracks).
- **User experience:** C-style comparison and ADR review; specialized branches reuse familiar worlds. An optional real-practice handoff explains model omissions and what authorized measurement must verify.
- **Functional scope:** one validated advanced problem at a time; current versioned certification/Applied Skills overlays as secondary views; readiness remains unavailable until sampling/calibration supports a bounded recommendation.
- **Technical deliverables:** synthetic cost/capacity/recovery fixtures, rubric/human review records, stale-mapping lifecycle and safe inert lab artifacts. Live metadata requires explicit auth/terms review; real-lab execution requires a separate security/cost design and authorization.
- **Automated verification:** arithmetic/fault/recovery witnesses, multiple feasible solutions, deliberate infeasibility, non-repeatable awards, content/evidence version compatibility and full quality gates. Never substitute synthetic costs/times for provider guarantees.
- **Human review:** Azure architecture specialist challenges assumptions, operator verifies runbook clarity, learners defend alternate designs and transfer changed constraints. Real practice, if later authorized, compares measured outcomes with simulation boundaries.
- **Acceptance:** different valid solutions pass hard constraints and receive reasoned review; no automatic prose/AI grading or exam-pass claims; source/credential requirements current; real-practice risk boundary explicit.
- **Exclusions:** autonomous cloud execution, copying official assessments, pricing guarantees, universal architecture score, unvalidated readiness estimate or uncontrolled AI/model integrations.

## Phase 4 — optional accounts and organizations

- **Learning outcomes:** preserve trustworthy evidence/history and support targeted practice in a professional setting without redefining activity as competence.
- **User experience:** private personal progress and explicit organizational membership/visibility; opt-in achievements only under comparable conditions. Training use does not require shared simulated infrastructure.
- **Functional scope:** only needs established by user research: multi-device persistence, instructor review, controlled reporting, export/deletion and retention. Collaborative worlds require a separate decision.
- **Technical deliverables:** separately authorized backend/auth, server-side attempt verification/award transactions, independent organization IDs, membership isolation and migration of local-unverified records without elevating trust.
- **Automated verification:** authorization/isolation/CSRF/session/rate-limit tests, transactional deduplication, deletion/export, tenant/organization confusion and untrusted import cases, plus existing simulation/learning gates.
- **Human review:** security/privacy/accessibility review and organizational fairness review with affected users. Define who may see individual performance and for what purpose before collection.
- **Acceptance:** consent/visibility controls work; no cross-organization data disclosure; source/evidence provenance is intact; optional leaderboards support opt-out/pseudonyms and do not reward speed or unequal assistance.
- **Exclusions:** employer/customer integrations/data by default, production provisioning without approval, public rankings, covert monitoring, using local scores for employment decisions or treating an Entra tenant as product authorization.

## Cross-phase stop conditions

Stop expansion when a technical rule lacks a defensible boundary/source, a required action is inaccessible, an unsupported operation appears successful, an independent result has hidden guidance, a persistence conflict loses work silently, or a source/mapping is stale but presented as current. Record and resolve the specific failure rather than filling the roadmap with more features. Preserve Git history, draft review and documentation continuity; no merge/production/new infrastructure without explicit owner authorization. Existing branch previews remain allowed.
